Stay Safe from Job Scams

Learn to identify and avoid fraudulent internship postings

⚠️ Important Warning

Legitimate companies will NEVER ask you to pay money upfront for a job opportunity. If someone asks for payment, it's almost certainly a scam.

🛡️ How to Protect Yourself

🔍

Research the Company

  • Check official website and social media
  • Look for company reviews on Glassdoor
  • Verify company registration details
  • Search for scam reports online
💬

Verify Communication

  • Use official company email domains
  • Avoid WhatsApp-only communication
  • Request video calls for interviews
  • Check recruiter's LinkedIn profile
💰

Never Pay Money

  • No registration or processing fees
  • No training or certification costs
  • No security deposits
  • No equipment or software purchases
📄

Review Job Details

  • Check for clear job description
  • Verify realistic salary expectations
  • Look for proper company address
  • Ensure professional communication

🚩 Major Red Flags

CRITICAL

Upfront Payment Required

Any request for money before starting work is a scam. This includes registration fees, training costs, background check fees, or equipment purchases.

HIGH RISK

Too Good to Be True

Unrealistic salaries (e.g., $5000/month for entry-level), no experience required, guaranteed positions, or instant offers without interviews.

HIGH RISK

Pressure Tactics

"Limited slots", "Offer expires in 24 hours", "Apply immediately", or other urgent language designed to rush your decision.

MEDIUM RISK

Unprofessional Communication

Poor grammar, spelling mistakes, generic emails, or communication only through personal messaging apps like WhatsApp.

MEDIUM RISK

Vague Company Information

No clear company name, missing address, no official website, or website with suspicious content.

MEDIUM RISK

Personal Information Requests

Asking for bank details, credit card info, or copies of sensitive documents before a formal offer.

📖 Real Scam Examples

SCAM

Example 1: Registration Fee Scam

"Congratulations! You've been selected for a Software Engineering Internship at Google. To confirm your position, please pay a one-time registration fee of $500 (fully refundable). Limited slots available!"

Why it's a scam:
  • Google never charges registration fees
  • No legitimate company asks for upfront payment
  • Uses pressure tactics ("limited slots")
  • Promises refund that never comes
SCAM

Example 2: WhatsApp-Only Scam

"Urgent hiring! Work from home, earn $3000/month. No experience needed. Contact on WhatsApp: +1234567890. Instant offer letter provided!"

Why it's a scam:
  • Only WhatsApp communication (no official channels)
  • Unrealistic salary for no experience
  • Urgency and pressure tactics
  • No company information provided
LEGITIMATE

Example 3: Real Internship

"Software Engineering Intern - Summer 2024. Work with our backend team on REST APIs. Requirements: CS degree, Python knowledge. Competitive stipend. Apply at careers.company.com"

Why it's legitimate:
  • Clear job description and requirements
  • Official application portal
  • Realistic expectations
  • No upfront payment requested

📢 What to Do If You Encounter a Scam

1

Don't Engage Further

Stop all communication immediately. Don't send money or personal information.

2

Document Everything

Take screenshots of messages, emails, and job postings as evidence.

3

Report to Authorities

File a complaint with local cybercrime police and consumer protection agencies.

4

Report to Platform

Report the posting on LinkedIn, Indeed, or wherever you found it.

5

Warn Others

Share your experience on social media and job seeker forums to protect others.
